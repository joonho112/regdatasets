Canadian women's labour-force participation

Source: R/data-womenlf.R
womenlf.Rd

Work status, husband's income, presence of children and region of residence for 263 married Canadian women, one row per woman. The data come from a 1977 survey by the Social Change in Canada Project of the York Institute for Social Research and are a textbook example in Fox (2016, 3rd ed.). They are the same data as carData::Womenlf on CRAN.

Usage

womenlf

Format

A tibble with 263 rows and 5 variables.

obs

Observation number. Type: integer.

husbinc

Husband's income. Units: thousands of dollars. Type: integer. The sources give the unit only as $1,000 and do not name the currency (the survey is Canadian).

region

Region of residence. Values: Atlantic = Atlantic Canada; BC = British Columbia; Ontario = Ontario; Prairie = Prairie provinces; Quebec = Quebec. Type: character. Stored as text.

workstat

Wife's work status. Values: 0 = not working outside the home; 1 = part time; 2 = full time. Type: integer.

chilpres

Children present in the household. Values: 0 = absent; 1 = present. Type: integer.

See codebook("womenlf") for this table as a data frame.

Source

Fox, J. (2016). Applied regression analysis and generalized linear models (3rd ed.). Sage. Data: Social Change in Canada Project, York Institute for Social Research (1977). https://cran.r-project.org/package=carData

Stata Press companion data of Rabe-Hesketh and Skrondal, via the UC Berkeley EDUC 275B course files; identical to carData::Womenlf on CRAN. Provenance: verified.

Details

The outcome workstat has three ordered categories: 0 = not working outside the home (155 women), 1 = part time (42) and 2 = full time (66). husbinc is the husband's income in thousands of dollars, chilpres is 1 when children are present in the household (184 women), and region is text with five values, which a model formula turns into a factor with Atlantic Canada as the reference level. obs is a row number, not a predictor. There are no missing values.

Version 1.0.0 changes no values. Earlier documentation named the wrong source and edition: the data come from the Social Change in Canada Project, not from a Survey of Consumer Finances, and the book is Fox (2016, 3rd ed.).

Suggested analyses.

  • binary logistic regression of working at all (workstat > 0) on husband's income and children;

  • a proportional odds model for the three ordered categories, with a check of the proportional odds assumption;

  • alternatives if that assumption fails: a multinomial logit, or two binary logits (working or not; full time or part time among women who work);

  • an interaction between husband's income and the presence of children.

See also

carData::Womenlf on CRAN holds the same 263 women in the same order, under other variable names and with work status, children and region as factors. satisfaction has another ordinal outcome. codebook() and list_datasets().

Examples

head(womenlf)
#> # A tibble: 6 × 5
#>     obs husbinc region  workstat chilpres
#>   <int>   <int> <chr>      <int>    <int>

#> 3     3      45 Ontario        0        1
#> 4     4      23 Ontario        0        1
#> 5     5      19 Ontario        0        1
#> 6     6       7 Ontario        0        1
table(womenlf$workstat)   # 0 = not working, 1 = part time, 2 = full time
#> 
#>   0   1   2 
#> 155  42  66 

# Working at all: binary logistic regression
glm(I(workstat > 0) ~ husbinc + chilpres, family = binomial, data = womenlf)
#> 
#> Call:  glm(formula = I(workstat > 0) ~ husbinc + chilpres, family = binomial, 
#>     data = womenlf)
#> 
#> Coefficients:
#> (Intercept)      husbinc     chilpres  
#>     1.33583     -0.04231     -1.57565  
#> 
#> Degrees of Freedom: 262 Total (i.e. Null);  260 Residual
#> Null Deviance:	    356.2 
#> Residual Deviance: 319.7 	AIC: 325.7

# Proportional odds model (requires MASS; summary() reports t-values
# without p-values)
if (requireNamespace("MASS", quietly = TRUE)) {
  fit_ord <- MASS::polr(factor(workstat) ~ husbinc + chilpres,
                        data = womenlf, Hess = TRUE)
  summary(fit_ord)
}
