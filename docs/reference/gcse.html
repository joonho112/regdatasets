GCSE and London Reading Test scores

Source: R/data-gcse.R
gcse.Rd

Examination results of 4,059 students in 65 inner-London schools, one row per student: the GCSE (General Certificate of Secondary Education) exam score at age 16 and the London Reading Test (LRT) score at age 11. The data are the Exam data of Goldstein et al. (1993), distributed by the Centre for Multilevel Modelling at the University of Bristol, with both scores multiplied by 10.

Usage

gcse

Format

A tibble with 4,059 rows and 5 variables.

school

School ID. Type: integer. 65 schools in inner London.

student

Student ID within school. Type: integer. Original Exam-data ID (1 to 913), not unique across schools. Four IDs occur twice in the same school (8 rows, different students), so it is not a reliable key.

gcse

GCSE exam score at age 16. Units: normalized score x 10. Type: double. Equals 10 x normexam in mlmRev::Exam (mean about 0, SD about 10). The stored model prediction pred was removed in v1.0.0.

lrt

London Reading Test score at age 11. Units: standardized score x 10. Type: double. Equals 10 x standLRT in mlmRev::Exam.

gender

Girl. Values: 0 = boy; 1 = girl. Type: integer.

See codebook("gcse") for this table as a data frame.

Source

Goldstein, H., Rasbash, J., Yang, M., Woodhouse, G., Pan, H., Nuttall, D., & Thomas, S. (1993). A multilevel analysis of school examination results. Oxford Review of Education, 19(4), 425-433. doi:10.1080/0305498930190401 https://www.bristol.ac.uk/cmm/learning/mmsoftware/data-rev.html

The Exam data of the Centre for Multilevel Modelling (Bristol), rescaled by 10, via the UC Berkeley EDUC 275B course files; also in the CRAN package mlmRev as Exam. Provenance: verified.

Details

Scores. gcse and lrt are the Exam data's normexam and standLRT (as named in mlmRev::Exam) multiplied by 10, so each has a mean of about 0 and a standard deviation of about 10: one point is about a tenth of a standard deviation. The data frame and its outcome share the name gcse, so the outcome is gcse$gcse.

Coding. gender is 1 for girls and 0 for boys. Thirty of the 65 schools are single-sex (20 girls' and 10 boys' schools), so in a model with a school factor the gender effect rests on the 35 mixed schools. school is an ID number from 1 to 65; the data do not name the schools. student is the original Exam ID: it is not unique across schools, and four IDs occur twice within the same school (8 rows, different students), so school and student together do not identify a row. No value is missing.

Changes in version 1.0.0. The column pred, a stored prediction from a multilevel model on the original score scale (not multiplied by 10), was removed, so the data now have 5 columns. The other values are unchanged.

Suggested analyses.

  • Simple regression of gcse on lrt, for all students or within one school.

  • ANCOVA: comparing schools, or girls and boys, at the same lrt score.

  • Interactions: does the lrt slope differ by gender, or across schools (lrt * factor(school))?

  • Nonlinearity, model building and diagnostics: a quadratic term in lrt, residual plots and influence measures. Because students are nested in schools, the data also suit random-intercept and random-slope models.

See also

mlmRev::Exam on CRAN has the same students on the original score scale, with more variables; codebook() and list_datasets().
