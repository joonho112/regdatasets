NELS:88 base year and first follow-up (2,000 students)

Source: R/data-nels_data.R
nels_data.Rd

Questionnaire items, composites and test scores for 2,000 students in the National Education Longitudinal Study of 1988 (NELS:88): 299 variables from the base year, 1988 (grade 8), and the first follow-up, 1990 (grade 10). One row per student. The NELS variables keep their original names and codes, for example bys12 (sex) and by2xmstd (grade-8 mathematics score). The file is a teaching extract of the NCES public-use data; how its 2,000 students were chosen is not documented. Because there are 299 variables, use codebook("nels_data") to search variables in R, or scroll below the variable list for Details and Examples.

Details

Layout. The variables are grouped by wave and kind: the base-year (1988) student questionnaire (148 items, bys2a to bys83j), tests, composites and school characteristics; then the first follow-up (1990) questionnaire (105 items, f1s8a to f1s110yr), one test score, and composites and status variables such as f1stat. The only 1990 test score is reading (f12xrstd), so change in achievement can be followed for reading only. id is the NELS student identifier (STU_ID in NCES files). year1 and year2 are not NELS variables and their meaning is not documented; leave them out of analyses.

Codes. Items keep their NELS codes and follow the NCES NELS:88 codebook wording, so check them before modeling. The 40 agreement items run from 1 = strongly agree to 4 = strongly disagree. Most yes/no items are 1 = yes and 2 = no, but bys74 (held back a grade) is 1 = no and 2 = yes. bys12 (sex) is 1 = male and 2 = female, and bys31a (race/ethnicity) runs from 1 = Asian or Pacific Islander to 5 = American Indian. Nominal items, such as occupations and school programs, need factor(). codebook("nels_data") returns the full variable table, with codes and units, as a data frame.

Missing data and version 1.0.0. In 0.2.0 most nonresponse codes were stored as numbers, so a 98 or a 99.98 could enter a model as if it were a real value. Version 1.0.0 recodes them to NA, 41,966 values in all (0.2.0 had already recoded 82 others, the 998 codes in g8minor and g8lunch):

  • NELS nonresponse codes such as 8 = missing, 9 = legitimate skip, 6 = multiple response and 7 = refusal, or 98, 99, 96 and 97 on items with more categories (40,145 cells);

  • non-integer codes from the NELS codebook in test scores and composites, such as 99.98 = missing and 99.99 = test not completed (1,356 cells);

  • "don't know" answers on the ordered items bypared, bys34a, bys34b and f1s17 (465 cells).

On nominal items, such as the occupation and school-program items, "don't know" is kept as its own category. The recoded_to_na column of codebook("nels_data") shows what was recoded in each variable.

Legitimate skips (questions that did not apply to a student) are NA as well, so NA does not always mean a missing answer: bys31c and bys31d, which ask Hispanic students about their origin and race, hold 1,717 legitimate skips each. The 109 students who did not take part in the 1990 follow-up (f1stat other than 0) have no 1990 questionnaire data. No student is complete on all 299 variables, so na.omit(nels_data) returns no rows; choose the variables first.

Sample. How the 2,000 students were drawn is not documented, and the file has no sampling weights, so results describe these students rather than the national population of eighth graders that NELS:88 sampled. All 296 named NELS variables (plus id, the NCES student ID STU_ID) are in the NCES public-use data; the remaining two columns (year1, year2) are undocumented.

Suggested analyses.

  • model building and variable selection, for example for the grade-8 mathematics score (by2xmstd) with SES (byses), bracketed homework time (byhomewk, 1 = none to 8 = 21+ hours/week), attitudes and school characteristics as candidate predictors;

  • regression diagnostics (residuals, leverage and influence) for such models;

  • change in reading between grade 8 and grade 10: f12xrstd on by2xrstd and other predictors;

  • logistic regression of a binary item, such as being held back a grade (bys74, recoded to 0/1, e.g. I(bys74 == 2)).
