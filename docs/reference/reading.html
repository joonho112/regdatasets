Reading comprehension experiment

Source: R/data-reading.R
reading.Rd

Reading-comprehension scores from a randomized experiment that compared three methods of teaching reading comprehension. Each row is one of 66 students, 22 of them randomly assigned to each method: Basal (the traditional method), DRTA (Directed Reading-Thinking Activity) and Strat (strategy instruction). The experiment was conducted by Baumann and Jones at Purdue University and reported by Moore and McCabe (1993).

Usage

reading

Format

A tibble with 66 rows and 8 variables.

subject

Student ID. Type: integer.

group

Teaching method. Values: Basal = traditional method; DRTA = Directed Reading-Thinking Activity (innovative); Strat = strategy instruction (innovative). Type: character. Same factor as method (Basal = 1, DRTA = 2, Strat = 3), stored as text; 22 students were randomly assigned to each.

pre1

Pretest 1: comprehension (intruded sentences). Units: score points. Type: integer.

pre2

Pretest 2: comprehension (measure 2). Units: score points. Type: integer.

post1

Posttest 1: comprehension (intruded sentences). Units: score points. Type: integer. Same measure as pre1.

post2

Posttest 2: comprehension (measure 2). Units: score points. Type: integer. Same measure as pre2.

post3

Posttest 3: comprehension (measure 3). Units: score points. Type: integer. A third measure with no pretest.

method

Teaching method (numeric code). Values: 1 = Basal; 2 = DRTA; 3 = Strat. Type: integer. Numeric version of group.

See codebook("reading") for this table as a data frame.

Source

Moore, D. S., & McCabe, G. P. (1993). Introduction to the practice of statistics (2nd ed., pp. 794-795). W. H. Freeman. Data from an experiment by Baumann and Jones (Purdue University). https://cran.r-project.org/package=carData

Via the UC Berkeley EDUC 275B course files; identical to carData::Baumann on CRAN. Provenance: verified.

Details

Design. Each student took two pretests before instruction and three post-tests after it. post1 repeats the measure of pre1 (intruded sentences) and post2 the measure of pre2; post3 is a third measure with no pretest. Because the methods were randomly assigned, differences between the groups' mean post-test scores estimate the effects of the methods, and the equal group sizes make the design balanced.

Coding. group and method carry the same factor: group as text ("Basal", "DRTA", "Strat") and method as a number (1, 2, 3). Use group, or factor(method); entering method as a number would fit a straight line across the three methods. lm() turns group into a factor with Basal, the traditional method, as the reference level. There are no missing values. Version 1.0.0 changed no values; it corrects the source cited in the v0.2.0 help page.

Suggested analyses.

  • one-way ANOVA as a regression with dummy (or effect) coding, and the overall F-test;

  • planned contrasts, e.g. Basal against the two innovative methods and DRTA against Strat, with adjustments for multiple comparisons;

  • ANCOVA: a post-test adjusted for its pretest, e.g. post1 ~ pre1 + group, with a check of parallel slopes (post1 ~ pre1 * group).

See also

carData::Baumann on CRAN has the same data, with group as a factor and the tests named pretest.1 to post.test.3. codebook() gives the variable table as a data frame, and list_datasets(), e.g. list_datasets(topic = "ancova"), lists other datasets suited to ANCOVA.
