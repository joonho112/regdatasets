Florida county crime rates

Source: R/data-crime.R
crime.Rd

Crime rate, median income, education and urbanization for the 67 counties of Florida, one row per county. They are the Florida county data of Agresti and Finlay's textbook Statistical Methods for the Social Sciences (2009), courtesy of L. Winner of the University of Florida. The year the figures refer to and the agency that compiled them are not documented.

Usage

crime

Format

A tibble with 67 rows and 5 variables.

county

County. Type: character. 67 Florida counties in capitals, at most 8 characters long, so 11 names are cut short (e.g., HILLSBOR = Hillsborough, PALM_B. = Palm Beach); Miami-Dade appears as DADE.

c

Crime rate. Units: crimes per 1,000 residents per year. Type: integer. The year and the agency behind the data are not documented in the sources.

i

Median income. Units: thousands of US dollars. Type: double.

hs

Adults with at least a high school education. Units: percent. Type: double.

u

Urban population. Units: percent of residents living in urban areas. Type: double.

See codebook("crime") for this table as a data frame.

Source

Agresti, A., & Finlay, B. (2009). Statistical methods for the social sciences (4th ed.). Pearson Prentice Hall. Florida crime data, courtesy of L. Winner, University of Florida. https://alanagresti.com/smss/data/Florida.dat

Textbook data file (Florida.dat), via the UC Berkeley EDUC 275B course files; also in the CRAN package smss as fl.crime. Provenance: verified.

Details

Variables. c is the crime rate (crimes per 1,000 residents per year), i the median income in thousands of US dollars, hs the percentage of adults with at least a high school education and u the percentage of residents living in urban areas.

Quirks. County names are in capitals and at most 8 characters long, so 11 are cut short (for example HILLSBOR is Hillsborough and PALM_B. is Palm Beach). Miami-Dade appears under its former name DADE, and Suwannee is spelled SUWANEE. Check the names before merging with other county data. Ten counties have no urban population (u = 0) and one has c = 0, which matters for log transformations. No value is missing, and the values are unchanged in version 1.0.0.

Confounding. On its own, hs has a positive slope (about 1.5 crimes per 1,000 residents per percentage point), but controlling for u turns it negative (about -0.6) and not statistically significant: urbanization is strongly correlated with both education (r = 0.79) and crime (r = 0.68). Likewise, the correlation of c and hs is 0.47, but their partial correlation controlling for u is -0.15. Income i shows the same reversal.

Suggested analyses.

  • Multiple regression with continuous predictors and omitted-variable bias: c ~ hs versus c ~ hs + u.

  • Partial correlation and added-variable plots.

  • An interaction between two continuous predictors, c ~ hs * u; centering hs and u first makes the main effects easier to interpret.

  • Nonlinear terms, such as a quadratic in u or the log of i.

See also

The CRAN package smss has the same county data as smss::fl.crime; codebook() and list_datasets().
