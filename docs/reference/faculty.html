Faculty salaries

Source: R/data-faculty.R
faculty.Rd

Academic-year salaries, rank, experience and gender of 514 faculty members of Bowling Green State University (Ohio) in 1993-94, one row per faculty member. The data were provided with DeMaris's (2004) book Regression with Social Data. The 514 cases are a subset defined by Rabe-Hesketh and Skrondal for Multilevel and Longitudinal Modeling Using Stata, and the file is identical to that book's Stata Press companion data.

Usage

faculty

Format

A tibble with 514 rows and 10 variables.

salary

Academic-year salary. Units: US dollars. Type: double. Nine-month academic-year salary, Bowling Green State University, 1993-94.

exprior

Years of prior experience (unverified). Units: years. Type: integer. Meaning not documented in the sources; the label is a guess from the variable name.

yearsbg

Years at BGSU (unverified). Units: years. Type: integer. Meaning not documented in the sources; years at BGSU is a guess. It cannot be years since the bachelor's degree (the old help text), because it is smaller than yearsdg in 257 rows.

yearsrank

Years in current rank (unverified). Units: years. Type: integer. Meaning not documented in the sources; the label is a guess from the variable name.

market

Marketability of the discipline. Units: ratio. Type: double. Ratio of the national average salary in the faculty member's discipline to the national average across all disciplines.

termdeg

Holds a terminal degree (unverified). Values: 0 = no; 1 = yes. Type: integer. Meaning not documented in the sources; the label is a guess from the variable name. Only 6 faculty are coded 0.

admin

Administrative appointment (unverified). Values: 0 = no; 1 = yes. Type: integer. Meaning not documented in the sources; the label is a guess from the variable name. 16 faculty are coded 1.

yearsdg

Years since degree. Units: years. Type: integer.

rank

Academic rank. Values: 1 = assistant professor; 2 = associate professor; 3 = full professor. Type: integer.

male

Male. Values: 0 = female; 1 = male. Type: integer.

See codebook("faculty") for this table as a data frame.

Source

DeMaris, A. (2004). Regression with social data: Modeling continuous and limited response variables. Wiley. doi:10.1002/0471677566 https://www.stata-press.com/data/mlmus4.html

514-case file of Rabe-Hesketh and Skrondal, Multilevel and Longitudinal Modeling Using Stata (Stata Press companion data), via the UC Berkeley EDUC 275B course files. Provenance: verified.

Details

Sample. The 514 faculty are not the whole university: the subset leaves out, among others, instructors, lecturers and faculty who are not on the graduate faculty. Rows 57 and 464 are identical on all ten variables; whether they are two people or one duplicated record is not documented. No value is missing, and the values are unchanged in version 1.0.0.

Variables. salary is the nine-month academic-year salary in US dollars. market is the ratio of the national average salary in the faculty member's discipline to the national average across disciplines, so values above 1 mark better-paid disciplines. yearsdg is years since degree; rank is 1 = assistant, 2 = associate and 3 = full professor (use factor(rank) in models); male is 1 for men and 0 for women.

Unverified variables. The sources do not define exprior, yearsbg, yearsrank, termdeg and admin; their labels are guesses from the names. yearsbg cannot be years since a bachelor's degree (the meaning given in version 0.2.0), because it is smaller than yearsdg in 257 rows. termdeg is 0 for only 6 faculty and admin is 1 for only 16. Do not base substantive conclusions on the labels of these five variables.

The gender gap. Men earned about 10,600 dollars more than women on average, but men make up 192 of the 211 full professors and 83 of the 143 assistant professors. Adjusting for rank, years since degree and market shrinks the gap to about 1,100 dollars, which is not statistically significant. Whether to adjust for rank is a substantive question, because rank may itself reflect the gender differences under study.

Suggested analyses.

  • The salary gap between men and women, before and after adjusting for factor(rank), yearsdg and market: dummy and continuous predictors in one model.

  • Interactions, for example whether the salary gain per year since degree differs between men and women (male * yearsdg).

  • log(salary) as the outcome: salary is mildly right-skewed, and on the log scale the coefficients are approximate proportional differences.

  • Collinearity: yearsdg, yearsbg, yearsrank and rank are correlated with one another (r from 0.49 to 0.84).
