High School and Beyond: five schools

Source: R/data-hsb_sub.R
hsb_sub.Rd

Mathematics achievement and background of 188 students in five schools of the High School and Beyond (HSB) file of Raudenbush and Bryk (2002), which holds 7,185 students in 160 schools from the U.S. High School and Beyond survey of the National Center for Education Statistics. Each row is a student; size and sector describe the student's school. The five schools are the ones with the lowest school IDs in that file.

Usage

hsb_sub

Format

A tibble with 188 rows and 7 variables.

minority

Minority student. Values: 0 = no; 1 = yes (nonwhite). Type: integer.

female

Female. Values: 0 = male; 1 = female. Type: integer.

ses

Socioeconomic status. Units: composite score. Type: double. Based on parental education, occupation and income.

mathach

Mathematics achievement score. Units: score points. Type: double.

size

School enrollment. Units: students. Type: integer. School level: the same for every student in a school.

sector

School sector. Values: 0 = public; 1 = Catholic. Type: integer. School level.

schoolid

School ID. Type: integer. The five schools with the lowest IDs in the HSB file: 1224, 1288, 1296, 1308 and 1317.

See codebook("hsb_sub") for this table as a data frame.

Source

Raudenbush, S. W., & Bryk, A. S. (2002). Hierarchical linear models: Applications and data analysis methods (2nd ed.). Sage. https://www.stata-press.com/data/mlmus4.html

The five lowest-numbered schools of the HSB file, as defined in Rabe-Hesketh and Skrondal (Stata Press), via the UC Berkeley EDUC 275B course files; the full file is also on CRAN (nlme::MathAchieve). Provenance: verified.

Details

Design. The five schools (IDs 1224, 1288, 1296, 1308 and 1317) have 47, 25, 48, 20 and 48 students. Schools 1224, 1288 and 1296 are public; schools 1308 and 1317 are Catholic. Because size and sector are constant within a school, they vary across only five schools: a sector comparison rests on three public and two Catholic schools, and a model with school dummies, factor(schoolid), cannot separate them from the school effects (R reports the aliased terms as NA). Students in the same school are not independent, so ordinary least-squares standard errors, especially for school-level predictors, can be too small.

Coding. minority, female and sector are 0/1 dummies (sector 1 = Catholic). There are no missing values, and version 1.0.0 changed no values.

Suggested analyses.

  • simple and multiple regression of mathach on ses, minority, female and sector;

  • interactions, e.g. whether the ses slope differs by sector (ses * sector);

  • model building and regression diagnostics in a small sample;

  • a first look at nested data: school dummies, factor(schoolid), or a separate ses slope in each school.

See also

hsbs1 for a different High School and Beyond extract (350 students with five test scores). The full 160-school file is on CRAN as nlme::MathAchieve (students) and nlme::MathAchSchool (schools). codebook() gives the variable table as a data frame, and list_datasets() lists the other datasets.
