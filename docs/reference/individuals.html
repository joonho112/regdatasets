Earnings, education and job sector (CPS 2000)

Source: R/data-individuals.R
individuals.Rd

Income, education, sex, age and job sector of 55,899 adults aged 25 to 65 from the March 2000 Current Population Survey (CPS), the survey of U.S. households that the Census Bureau conducts for the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Each row is a person. The six-variable extract of the CPS Annual Demographic Supplement was made by an unknown party, and the rule that selected these 55,899 people is not documented.

Usage

individuals

Format

A tibble with 55,899 rows and 6 variables.

id

Respondent ID. Type: integer.

age

Age. Units: years. Type: integer. The extract covers ages 25 to 65 only.

educ

Education level. Values: 1 = no high school; 2 = some high school; 3 = high school diploma; 4 = some college; 5 = bachelor's degree; 6 = postgraduate degree. Type: integer. The v0.2.0 help page gave wrong meanings for these codes.

sex

Sex. Values: 1 = male; 2 = female. Type: integer.

earn

Total annual income from all sources. Units: US dollars. Type: integer. Named earn, but the course materials describe it as total income from all sources. 170 values are negative, which is possible in CPS income (e.g., business losses).

job

Job sector. Values: 5 = private sector; 6 = government; 7 = self-employed. Type: integer. Codes start at 5. The v0.2.0 help page swapped codes 5 and 6.

See codebook("individuals") for this table as a data frame.

Source

U.S. Census Bureau. (2000). Current Population Survey: Annual demographic file, 2000 (Data set). ICPSR 3048. doi:10.3886/ICPSR03048.v1

Extract of unknown authorship, via the UC Berkeley EDUC 275B course files. Provenance: probable.

Details

Coding. educ, sex and job are integer codes: wrap them in factor() (e.g. factor(educ), factor(job)) or create 0/1 dummies so R does not treat category codes as numeric trends. job starts at 5: 5 = private sector, 6 = government, 7 = self-employed. sex is 1 = male, 2 = female, and educ runs from 1 = no high school to 6 = postgraduate degree (see the Format section). The v0.2.0 help page swapped job codes 5 and 6 and gave wrong meanings for the educ codes, so code written for it may treat government workers as private-sector workers. Version 1.0.0 corrected the documentation; the data are unchanged.

Income. Despite its name, earn is total annual income from all sources, in US dollars. Nobody has an income of exactly 0, and 170 values are negative (down to -24,998), which CPS income allows, for example through business losses; log(earn) is undefined for them. There are no missing values.

Suggested analyses.

  • interactions between two dummy variables, e.g. sex by private versus government sector, where the interaction coefficient is a difference in differences;

  • two-way ANOVA as regression with unequal cell sizes, and the difference between sequential (Type I) and adjusted (Type II or III) sums of squares;

  • dummy coding of the six education levels.

See also

codebook() for the variable table as a data frame, and list_datasets(), e.g. list_datasets(topic = "interactions"), for other datasets suited to interactions.

Examples

head(individuals)
#> # A tibble: 6 × 6
#>      id   age  educ   sex  earn   job
#>   <int> <int> <int> <int> <int> <int>
#> 1     1    25     2     2  7234     5
#> 2     2    25     5     1 37413     5
#> 3     3    25     4     2 29500     5
#> 4     4    25     3     2 13500     5
#> 5     5    25     4     1 17660     6
#> 6     6    25     4     1 37250     5

# Ages 30 to 39, postgraduate degree, private sector or government
sub <- subset(individuals,
              educ == 6 & age >= 30 & age <= 39 & job %in% c(5, 6))
sub$female  <- as.integer(sub$sex == 2)  # sex: 1 = male, 2 = female
sub$private <- as.integer(sub$job == 5)  # job: 5 = private, 6 = government

# Mean income in the four cells
with(sub, tapply(earn, list(female = female, private = private), mean))
#>       private
#> female        0        1
#>      0 50408.18 80003.42
#>      1 41548.72 54588.71

# The interaction coefficient is a difference in differences
lm(earn ~ female * private, data = sub)
#> 
#> Call:
#> lm(formula = earn ~ female * private, data = sub)
#> 
#> Coefficients:
#>    (Intercept)          female         private  female:private  
#>          50408           -8859           29595          -16555  
#>
