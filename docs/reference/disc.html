School discipline: NELS:88 original items

Source: R/data-disc.R
disc.Rd

Whether 2,000 eighth graders in the National Education Longitudinal Study of 1988 (NELS:88) had been sent to the office for misbehaving, with their sex, race/ethnicity, parents' education, whether they had their own bedroom and how often they had discussed class work with their parents. One row per student, from the 1988 base-year student questionnaire. The seven NELS items (bys*) keep their original NELS codes, next to 0/1 recodes ready for regression. The students are the same, and in the same order, as in disc2.

Usage

disc

Format

A tibble with 2,000 rows and 15 variables.

bys55a

Sent to the office for misbehaving. Values: 0 = never; 1 = once or twice; 2 = more than twice. Type: integer. Codes recoded to NA: 8 (missing): 28. NELS item BYS55A: how often this happened in the first semester of the base-year (8th-grade) school year.

bys12

Sex of respondent. Values: 1 = male; 2 = female. Type: integer. Codes recoded to NA: 8 (missing): 20. NELS item BYS12.

bys31a

Race/ethnic background. Values: 1 = Asian or Pacific Islander; 2 = Hispanic; 3 = Black, non-Hispanic; 4 = White, non-Hispanic; 5 = American Indian. Type: integer. Codes recoded to NA: 6 (multiple response): 6; 7 (refusal): 6; 8 (missing): 12. NELS item BYS31A; the source of race.

bys34a

Father's highest level of education. Values: 1 = did not finish high school; 2 = graduated high school; 3 = junior college; 4 = college, less than 4 years; 5 = graduated college; 6 = master's degree; 7 = Ph.D., M.D. or other advanced degree. Type: integer. Codes recoded to NA: 8 don't know: 249; 97 (refusal): 5; 98 (missing): 28. NELS item BYS34A (student report). Don't know (8) and missing (98) were already NA in v0.2.0; only the refusals (97) are new NAs in v1.0.0.

bys34b

Mother's highest level of education. Values: 1 = did not finish high school; 2 = graduated high school; 3 = junior college; 4 = college, less than 4 years; 5 = graduated college; 6 = master's degree; 7 = Ph.D., M.D. or other advanced degree. Type: integer. Codes recoded to NA: 8 don't know: 190; 97 (refusal): 3; 98 (missing): 15. NELS item BYS34B (student report). Don't know (8) and missing (98) were already NA in v0.2.0; only the refusals (97) are new NAs in v1.0.0.

bys35p

Has own bedroom. Values: 1 = has own bedroom; 2 = does not have one. Type: integer. Codes recoded to NA: 6 (multiple response): 1; 8 (missing): 32. NELS item BYS35P.

bys36c

Discussed things studied in class with parents. Values: 1 = not at all; 2 = once or twice; 3 = three or more times. Type: integer. Codes recoded to NA: 6 (multiple response): 1; 8 (missing): 30. NELS item BYS36C: since the start of the school year, with either or both parents or guardians.

sentoff

Ever sent to the office for misbehaving. Values: 0 = never; 1 = once or more. Type: integer. Derived from bys55a (1 = codes 1 and 2).

male

Male. Values: 0 = female; 1 = male. Type: integer. Derived from bys12.

race

Race/ethnicity. Values: Asian; Hispanic; Black; White; American Indian. Type: factor. Factor built from bys31a (Asian = Asian or Pacific Islander). The American Indian level was added in v1.0.0 for 68 students who were NA before; the 24 nonrespondents are NA.

fath_ed

Father finished high school. Values: 0 = did not finish high school; 1 = high school graduate or more. Type: integer. Derived from bys34a (0 = code 1; 1 = codes 2 to 7).

moth_ed

Mother finished high school. Values: 0 = did not finish high school; 1 = high school graduate or more. Type: integer. Derived from bys34b (0 = code 1; 1 = codes 2 to 7).

bedroom

Has own bedroom. Values: 0 = no; 1 = yes. Type: integer. Derived from bys35p (1 = code 1).

discuss

Does not have own bedroom (misnamed discuss). Values: 0 = has own bedroom; 1 = does not have own bedroom. Type: integer. Despite its name, discuss equals 1 - bedroom in every row (same 33 NAs) and is not derived from bys36c. Use bys36c for discussing class work with parents.

osentoff

Times sent to the office for misbehaving. Values: 0 = never; 1 = once or twice; 2 = more than twice. Type: integer. Same as bys55a; kept for ordinal models.

See codebook("disc") for this table as a data frame.

Source

National Center for Education Statistics. (1988). National Education Longitudinal Study of 1988 (NELS:88), base-year student data (Public-use data file). U.S. Department of Education. https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/nels88/

Teaching extract of 2,000 NELS:88 students prepared for UC Berkeley GSE courses (EDUC 293A/275B). Provenance: verified.

Details

Coding. The bys* items keep NELS codes: bys12 (sex) is 1 = male and 2 = female, and bys35p is 1 = has own bedroom and 2 = does not. The recodes are ready for regression: sentoff (0/1) and osentoff (0 = never, 1 = once or twice, 2 = more than twice) for the outcome, and male, fath_ed and moth_ed (parent finished high school), bedroom and the factor race as predictors. The outcome is the student's own report of how often they were sent to the office in the first semester of eighth grade; 552 of the 1,972 students who answered (28%) were sent at least once.

discuss is misnamed. Despite its name, discuss equals 1 - bedroom in every row (1 = does not have own bedroom); it has nothing to do with discussing school with parents. Use bys36c for how often the student discussed class work with parents.

race has five levels: Asian (Asian or Pacific Islander, 122 students), Hispanic (259), Black (194), White (1,333) and American Indian (68); the 24 nonrespondents are NA. The first level, Asian, is the default reference; use relevel(race, ref = "White") to compare each group with White students.

Missing data and version 1.0.0. Version 1.0.0 recoded the nonresponse codes left in the bys* items (missing, refusal and multiple response; 144 cells) to NA. On parents' education, "don't know" (8) and missing (98) were already NA in 0.2.0; only the 97 = refusal codes (5 fathers, 3 mothers) are new NAs. The recodes were already NA for all these students. Also new in 1.0.0 is the American Indian level of race: these 68 students were NA in 0.2.0, so models with race now include them. Parents' education is unknown for many students (282 fathers, 208 mothers), mostly because the student answered "don't know", and models with fath_ed or moth_ed lose them.

The seven bys* items match the same items in nels_data row by row, so the rows line up with that file as well.

Suggested analyses.

  • simple and multiple logistic regression of sentoff on sex, race and parents' education, with odds ratios;

  • likelihood-ratio tests, for example for the four race contrasts together;

  • a proportional odds model for the three-category osentoff;

  • how listwise deletion changes the sample when fath_ed (282 NAs) enters a model.

Ethical context

These data invite models with race as a predictor of school discipline. Racial disparities in school discipline are well documented in educational research and are often discussed in terms of the "school-to-prison pipeline". Here the outcome is the student's own report of being sent to the office, not a school record, and a race coefficient describes a difference in those reports; it does not explain it. The race factor follows the 1988 NELS questionnaire item (Asian or Pacific Islander, Hispanic, Black, White, American Indian), whose categories are neither exhaustive nor current descriptions of racial and ethnic identity. Before version 1.0.0 the 68 American Indian students were NA and so were dropped from every model that used race; they now have their own level, though estimates for so small a group are imprecise. Analyses are best read alongside the research literature on structural inequities in school discipline.

See also

disc2 for the same students with SES and test scores; nels_data for 299 variables on the same students; codebook() and list_datasets().
